What the connectivity test is

On the same computer, "can I open this site" has no single answer: Baidu and Taobao use domestic routes in China, Google and YouTube go through a cross-border exit, and ChatGPT and Claude also depend on whether your routing rules include their domains. A proxy makes it more complicated. The proxy client sends each domain down either the "direct" or the "proxy" path by rule, and one missing rule or the wrong mode leads to cases like "Google opens, ChatGPT does not" or "everything abroad is fine, but domestic sites got slower".

The test is simple: your browser requests a set of representative sites one by one and records which connect and how long each takes. A single site says little; comparing groups is what matters: China all reachable and international timing out means the proxy is off or not working; international fine but AI failing means your routing rules are missing domains; domestic slower than international means domestic traffic is going through the proxy too.

What this check does

  1. Send each site one warm-up request that is not counted, to keep one-time costs like DNS resolution and the TCP and TLS handshakes out of the result.
  2. Then run 6 rounds, each a HEAD request; no response within 3 seconds counts as a timeout. Rounds are spaced by a short random gap so they don't collide with requests to other sites.
  3. At most 6 sites run at once. Dozens of simultaneous requests compete for bandwidth and inflate the measured latency.
  4. Latency for each site is the median of its successful rounds. A site whose warm-up and first two rounds all fail is marked unreachable, and the remaining rounds are skipped.
  5. Count reachable sites and median latency per group, then draw a conclusion from how the groups compare.

Requests are sent in no-cors mode: the browser won't let a page read a cross-site response, but whether a response arrived and how long it took is visible, and that is enough. Whether the site returns 200, 404 or 403 does not matter; any response means the route works. Probe targets are small files served straight from the CDN edge, such as favicons, generate_204 and /cdn-cgi/trace, so the result mostly reflects the route rather than how busy the origin is. Requests carry no cookies or Referer, so the sites cannot tell they came from this page.

Reading the results

  • Dots: one per round. Darker green is faster, amber is over 250ms, red is a failed round, and a hollow circle is a round skipped after the site was marked unreachable. Click a site tile to see each round's latency, failure reason and probe URL.
  • Latency: median of successful rounds; anything over 999ms shows as 999ms.
  • Good / OK / Slow: median latency within 100ms, within 250ms, or above 250ms.
  • Unstable: some rounds succeeded and some failed. An occasional failure may just be jitter; failing more than half usually means heavy packet loss.
  • Unreachable: never connected. Expand the tile to see why: timeout, connection failure, or an instant failure (usually a browser extension blocking the request, not a network problem).
  • Group summary: the median in "reachable x/N · median Yms" is the median of the group's site latencies, which is less skewed by a few slow sites than the mean.

"Reachable" only means the network connects; it does not mean the service works. ChatGPT and Claude may connect and still refuse sign-in because your exit IP is in an unsupported region; check that with the AI access check. To see which exit each kind of traffic uses, try the split routing test.

FAQ

Why is the latency here higher than ping?
A web page cannot send ICMP. This page measures the time from sending a full HTTPS request to receiving the response headers, which includes the server's processing time and is usually a few to a few dozen milliseconds above ping. Each site first gets an uncounted warm-up request to keep DNS resolution and the TLS handshake out, later rounds reuse the same connection, and the median of successful rounds is used, so occasional jitter does not skew the result.
Why are domestic sites in China slower than international ones?
The most common cause is a proxy in "global mode": traffic to Baidu and Taobao is sent to a node abroad first and then back into China, so latency is much higher than a direct connection, and domestic services may treat you as an overseas user. Switch the proxy client to "rule" or "bypass mainland China" mode so domestic domains connect directly, then retest to see the difference.
Google connects but ChatGPT and Claude are unreachable. Why?
Most likely your routing rules are missing the AI service domains, so they go direct. Rule sets that include openai.com but not chatgpt.com, api.openai.com, anthropic.com or claude.ai are common. Note also that "reachable" only means the network connects; whether the service works also depends on whether your exit IP's region is supported, which the AI access check on this site covers.
Does "unreachable" always mean the site is blocked?
No. Ad blockers and privacy extensions often block requests to domains such as Facebook and X outright; these failures happen almost instantly, and clicking the site tile shows the reason. Corporate or school firewalls and outages on the site itself also look like unreachable. Only a whole-group pattern such as "China all reachable, international timing out" can be attributed to the cross-border route with reasonable confidence.
Does the test send my data to judgeip.com or to these sites?
Nothing goes to judgeip.com: every request goes straight from your browser to each site, results are shown only on this page, and this site's server receives none of them. To those sites it looks like an ordinary visit, the same as opening them yourself. Probe requests carry no cookies or Referer, so the sites cannot tell they came from this page or link them to your account there. The site icons on the tiles are loaded by your browser from the favicon.is icon service, also without a Referer; that service sees your IP and which sites' icons were requested.
