What split routing is

Proxy apps such as Clash, Surge, Shadowrocket and sing-box usually have three modes: direct (nothing through the proxy), global (everything through the proxy) and rule (decided per domain or IP owner). Rule mode is what "split routing" means: China-based sites matching rules such as GEOSITE,CN or GEOIP,CN connect directly and everything else goes through a proxy node. More careful setups assign separate nodes for AI, streaming and crypto, for example pinning ChatGPT and Claude to a US residential IP and Binance to Singapore.

With many rules, the config file alone won't tell you what actually happens: missing domains, rule order and where DNS resolution takes place can all send a site out through an unexpected exit. The direct way to check is to ask the site itself: the IP each site sees is the exit your traffic actually used to reach it.

What this check does

  1. For each site on the list, your browser sends a cross-origin request directly, without cookies or a referrer.
  2. For sites behind Cloudflare, request /cdn-cgi/trace and read ip (how it sees you), loc (the country of that IP) and colo (the Cloudflare data center you hit).
  3. The NetEase and ByteDance rows request a speed-test file on their CDN and read the visitor address echoed in a response header. They only return an IP; the country is taken from other sites that see the same exit (same IP, or same /64 for IPv6).
  4. 8 at a time, up to 6 seconds each; failed sites are retried once after 2 seconds and once after 4. If the main domain fails, a fallback domain is asked: perfops.cloudflareperf.com for the Cloudflare China network and unpkg.com for unpkg.
  5. Group results by exit IP, compare the China group with the international group and the AI group with other international sites, and draw a conclusion. IPv4 and IPv6 are only compared within the same family, so a dual-stack network is not mistaken for split routing. IPv6 is grouped by /64: a device's temporary addresses rotate within the range, and some proxies change the source address per connection, but it is still one exit.

All requests go straight from your browser; this site's server is not involved and receives no results. The only server-provided value is the "Address seen by the JudgeIP server" row, the address you used to reach this site when the page loaded, shown for comparison with what other sites see.

Reading the results

  • Split routing works: China-based sites see an exit in China and international sites see one abroad. This is what rule mode should look like.
  • All sites use one exit: no split routing. An exit in China means no proxy at all; an exit abroad means the proxy is in global mode. If you are outside China without a proxy, this is normal.
  • China-based sites also go through the proxy: domestic sites are routed through a node abroad. Access gets slower and some domestic services will treat you as an overseas user. Check the domestic direct rules in your config.
  • AI traffic routed separately: AI sites use a different exit from other international sites, so your AI-specific rules are working.
  • AI exit in an unsupported region: AI sites see an exit in mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau, Russia or similar. ChatGPT and Claude are not available there; switch nodes.
  • IPv4 and IPv6 use different exits: common when the proxy only handles IPv4 and sites that support IPv6 bypass it.

FAQ

"All sites use one exit": does that mean the proxy isn't working?
Not necessarily. It only means these dozens of sites see the same address: either no proxy at all (the exit is your local ISP) or a proxy in global mode (the exit is the proxy node). The exit's country tells them apart: mainland China means the former, abroad means the latter. To connect domestic sites directly and route international ones through the proxy, switch apps like Clash, Surge or Shadowrocket to "rule" mode.
What happens if China-based sites see a foreign IP?
Domestic sites are reached via a node abroad and back, so they get noticeably slower. Video, music, online banking and other domestic services judge you as an overseas user by IP, which can mean unavailable licensed content, extra sign-in verification or even risk-control flags. Usually the proxy app is in global mode, or the rules are missing domestic domains or direct rules such as GEOIP,CN.
The AI exit is in Hong Kong. Why does it say unsupported?
OpenAI and Anthropic do not serve mainland China, Hong Kong or Macau, and Russia, Belarus, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Cuba and others are also off the list. With an exit in one of these regions, pages may not open, sign-ups may be refused, and existing accounts may get flagged. This page only gives a short warning based on that common list; see the AI access check on this site for each service's full list of supported regions.
Why do some sites show "IP header not exposed" or "connection failed"?
The NetEase and ByteDance rows read the IP from a response header, which the browser only lets a page read if the site explicitly exposes it (Access-Control-Expose-Headers); otherwise this message is shown. The other sites read Cloudflare's /cdn-cgi/trace; a failure usually means the site is blocked on your network, timed out, or no longer uses Cloudflare. Failed sites are retried twice automatically, and a few failures do not affect the overall conclusion.
Does this site store the split routing results?
No. Each request goes straight from your browser to the site, without cookies or a referrer, and the addresses read are shown only on this page and never sent back to this site. The "Address seen by the JudgeIP server" row is the connection address this site saw when you opened the page; this page is not cached, and that address is not stored with the results.
